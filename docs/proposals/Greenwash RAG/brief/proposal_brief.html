Greenwash-RAG: Evidence-Grounded, Cross-Year Tracking of SGX Sustainability Claims

AI6127 Deep Neural Networks for NLP · Group project proposal, draft for feedback · 13 September 2026
In one paragraph. Given a company and a sustainability commitment or aspect (e.g. "Scope 1 emissions"), the system retrieves the company's own sustainability-report evidence across years, labels each statement implemented / planning / indeterminate following the A3CG scheme (ACL 2025), and returns a page-cited commitment timeline. The research question is whether retrieved evidence changes and improves A3CG-style action classification compared with classifying a sentence in isolation, which is how every published A3CG result works. The output is evidence-backed risk screening, never a verdict that a company is greenwashing.

1. Problem and motivation

Every SGX mainboard issuer publishes an annual sustainability report (Listing Rule 711A), typically 40–150 pages. Analysts, investors, regulators and journalists need to answer questions such as "Company X committed to an emissions target in 2021; was it delivered, and on which pages is the evidence?" Doing this by hand means reading three to four years of heavily templated reports. The target output looks like this (illustrative, fictional quotes):

2021  p.34  "we aim to reduce ... by 2025"            → planning
2022  p.41  "we remain committed to this target"      → planning
2023  p.38  "we continue to explore options"          → indeterminate
2024  —     aspect no longer mentioned
⚠ Risk signal: commitment stayed planning / indeterminate for 3 years with no implemented evidence
  (assistive screening, not a verdict)

Existing approaches handle this poorly for three reasons:

  1. Sentence-level judgement lacks context. "We have achieved this target ahead of schedule" does not say which target; "Industry players are expected to reduce plastic packaging" does not say whether the company itself acts. These match the dominant error types A3CG reports: ellipsis for supervised models and unattributed statements for LLMs.
  2. ESG language is templated. Near-identical sentences recur across companies, so similarity search without company and year constraints tends to return other issuers' passages.
  3. No prior work compares across years. A3CG, ML-Promise and EmeraldMind all work on single statements or single reports.

2. Research questions and positioning

  • RQ1 (headline). Does retrieved report-internal context (local, same-report, cross-year) make aspect–action classification more accurate than sentence-only input, on seen and unseen aspect categories?
  • RQ2. Can cross-year retrieval reconstruct a commitment's trajectory (planning → implemented, stuck at planning, weakened, dropped)?
  • RQ3. Do company/year metadata constraints, and a company-scoped temporal claim graph, reduce wrong-company retrieval compared with hybrid similarity search?

Out of scope: any company-level "is this greenwashing" verdict.

3. Task definition

  • Input: (company, aspect or claim text), optional year range.
  • Knowledge base: sustainability reports of SGX-listed issuers, 2020–2025, parsed into sentences and passages with {company, year, page, section} metadata. External news or regulator pages are an optional extension only.
  • Output: (1) an evidence set with company, year and page; (2) a label per statement–aspect pair; (3) a cross-year trajectory for the aspect; (4) risk signals (vague wording, missing target year or number, internal inconsistency), each with page citations and an "assistive screening, not a verdict" notice.
LabelDefinition (A3CG wording, condensed)
implementedAn action has already been taken to address the aspect (in place, advanced or achieved).
planningAn action is planned or a commitment is made by the company (including "strive to").
indeterminateUnclear whether or how the company addresses the aspect: hedged or unattributed wording, disclosure of figures only, statements of belief or risk. Not evidence of greenwashing.
noneThe sentence has no sustainability aspect, or the highlighted phrase is not one (A3CG's no-aspect class).

4. Data

4.1 Knowledge base (collected 10 September 2026)

ReportsIssuersYearsPages / wordsPassages
240 sustainability reports56 SGX-listed companies, REITs and trusts; 41 with ≥3 years, 29 with all six 2020–2025 (38–43 per year)10,635 pages / 3.48 M words ~11.6k at 300 words (course minimum 10k); 108,948 parsed sentences

Reports were fetched from the companies' own investor-relations sites at ≤1 request/s, with source URL and fetch date recorded per file; an 11-step filter removed annual reports, AGM notices, GRI indexes, non-English editions and duplicates, keeping one report per company-year. Report aggregators were used only to discover which reports exist. The PDFs remain the issuers' copyright and are not redistributed; only derived passages and metadata are shared as the course requires.

4.2 Evaluation set (eval.xls, 1,000 sentences)

  • Sampling. 1,000 sentences from 52 issuers' 2023–2025 reports, each with company, year, page and surrounding context. Each sentence carries one highlighted candidate aspect and its A3CG category (10 categories), or no aspect.
  • Labelling. A single four-way decision per row (implemented / planning / indeterminate / none), using a guideline written from A3CG's definitions and indeterminate sub-rules. Candidate aspects and a pre-label were produced by an LLM; human annotators label blind to the pre-label. Among rows with an aspect, the pre-label split (implemented 51 %, indeterminate 27 %, planning 22 %) resembles A3CG's (54 % / 32 % / 14 %); 20 % of rows have no aspect, against 33 % of A3CG statements.
  • Agreement. A pilot of 50 sentences with 6 annotators is under way, with Fleiss' κ and raw agreement reported; the full set then gets at least two independent annotators per row plus adjudication, to meet the course's ≥80 % agreement requirement.
  • Seen/unseen. The category column lets us map A3CG's three fold-wise category splits onto our set.
  • Retrieval qrels. ≥50 queries (company-specific claim lookup, cross-year same-aspect lookup, generic) with pooled top-10 manual relevance judgements.

5.1 Evidence conditions (the RQ1 ablation)

The same classifier and prompt run under each condition, so differences come from the evidence alone:

ConditionEvidence given to the classifier
sentence_onlyThe target sentence (A3CG setting, the baseline)
localPlus the neighbouring sentences from the same report
same_reportPlus top-k passages retrieved from the same report (hybrid retrieval)
cross_year (hybrid)Plus top-k passages from the same company's other years (hybrid retrieval with filters)
cross_year (graph / fused)Plus statements reached through the temporal claim graph, with text fallback
cross_year (agentic, stretch)A bounded plan–retrieve–stop loop (≤3 hops) over the same tools, with cost and latency reported

5.2 Components

  • Temporal claim graph. A narrow, mostly deterministic graph (DuckDB + networkx): companies → reports → statements → aspects, with follows_up edges linking the same aspect across years. Cross-year links are marked when the sentence is a verbatim repeat, so trajectory analysis can separate "the claim progressed" from "the paragraph was copied". No open-domain entity extraction and no community summaries.
  • Classifiers. A zero-shot LLM classifier with A3CG definitions (shared by all systems), and a model fine-tuned on A3CG as the supervised reference (the GRACE checkpoints released by the A3CG authors).
  • Cross-year consistency. Label transitions per (company, aspect) across years, plus structured target extraction (target year, baseline year, Scope 1/2/3 and units) to detect moved or dropped targets.
  • Risk-signal explanation. Rules for vague wording and missing numbers or years, plus a cited LLM explanation; always labelled as screening.
  • Optional. Visual page retrieval on a capped table/chart subset (≤1,200 pages); external evidence only as a stretch goal.

7. Current status and early observations

  • Done: corpus collection and filtering; sentence parsing with provenance (all 1,000 eval sentences map back to a parsed report sentence); the 1,000-sentence evaluation sheet and pilot annotation files; a shared evaluation harness (frozen dataset snapshots, per-condition metrics, significance tests, run manifests) with a FastAPI interface for the UI; a first temporal claim graph (52 issuers, 233 reports, 2,804 cross-year aspect chains).
  • Templated reporting is measurable. About 27 % of cross-year links join a sentence repeated verbatim a year later, which both motivates RQ2 and means cross-year evidence must skip verbatim repeats.
  • Aspect normalisation is hard. Merging differently worded aspects with embeddings plus LLM verification reached only 0.60 pairwise precision on a held-out check, so the graph uses conservative normalisation and matches aspects at query time.
  • Proof of concept on 300 items development numbers Scored against the LLM pre-labels (not human gold), the zero-shot classifier reaches macro-F1 ≈0.77–0.80 under every condition; the cross-year graph condition neither helps nor hurts beyond run-to-run variation (the same prompt re-sampled changes 9 % of labels). The pipeline therefore runs end to end, and the final ablation must use repeated runs and bucketed analysis rather than a single run.

8. Main risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
Reaching ≥80 % inter-annotator agreement on implemented vs indeterminate Pilot round before scaling; guideline quotes A3CG's indeterminate sub-rules; "unsure" flag and adjudication
Evidence effect is small relative to classifier variance Repeated runs, flip analysis against a sentence_only-vs-itself baseline, error-type buckets; report nulls honestly
PDF extraction noise (tables, multi-column layouts) Sentence filter against GRI index and page furniture; A3CG's own released text shows similar noise is tolerable
Reputational sensitivity of naming companies Risk-screening framing throughout; no company-level verdicts in the report or UI
